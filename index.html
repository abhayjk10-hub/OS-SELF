    SCHEDULING FIELD GUIDE

    Eight ways to share
    one processor.

    Same hardware, different philosophies. Explore each selection rule and the trade-offs it creates.

    YOUR CURRENT PICK

    How preemptive priority works

    The CPU selects the ready process with the smallest priority number. It checks again at each arrival, allowing a higher-priority process to preempt the running process.

    01

    Preemptive Priority

    The most important ready process takes over—even if another process is already running.

    HIGH PRIORITY CAN INTERRUPT
    02

    Non-Preemptive Priority

    Choose the most important process, then let it finish before picking again.

    FINISH WHAT YOU START
    03

    First Come, First Served

    Processes are handled in arrival order. Simple and fair, but a long job can hold up the line.

    ARRIVAL ORDER WINS
    04

    Shortest Job First

    Give the CPU to the shortest waiting job. Efficient, but long jobs may have to wait.

    SMALLEST BURST FIRST
    05

    Round Robin

    Give everyone a small turn, then move unfinished work to the back of the queue.

    SHARE THE CPU IN TURNS
    06

    Shortest Remaining Time First

    Preempt the running process whenever a new arrival has less work remaining.

    PREEMPTIVE SJF · LOW AVERAGE WAIT
    07

    Highest Response Ratio Next

    Choose the largest (waiting + service) / service ratio to balance short jobs and long-waiting jobs.

    NON-PREEMPTIVE · STARVATION AWARE
    08

    Multilevel Feedback Queue

    New work starts at the top. Jobs that use their full time slice move to lower-priority queues with longer turns.
